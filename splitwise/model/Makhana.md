## 1. Raw Material Selection

**Goal:** Choose high-expansion, low-moisture makhana seeds.

|Parameter|Ideal Range|
|---|---|
|Size|14–16 mm (uniform)|
|Moisture|≤ 12 %|
|Source|North Bihar (Darbhanga, Madhubani, Saharsa)|
|Reject|Cracked, burnt, uneven-size seeds|

_Tip:_ Buy graded “Phool Makhana 2A” or “3A” quality for uniform roasting.

## 2. Roasting Process (Core Step)

### Equipment Options

- **Traditional:** Iron wok (kadhai) on wood/gas flame.
- **Industrial:** Rotary roaster or air roaster (preferred for uniform heating & scale).

### Temperature & Timing

|Stage|Temp (°C)|Time|Output|
|---|---|---|---|
|Pre-heat seeds|180 °C|2–3 min|Seeds dry out evenly|
|Roast|220–250 °C|3–5 min|Pop opens and becomes crisp|
|Cool|Room temp|10 min|Moisture stabilization|

**Note:** Don’t overheat → it causes bitterness & dark color.  
	Uniform heat distribution is crucial — rotate continuously.

## 3. Flavoring / Seasoning

After roasting, while makhana is slightly warm:

### Coating Base

- **Medium:** Ghee, olive oil, or rice-bran oil (2–3 % by weight)
- **Binder:** Maltodextrin or gum arabic (for dry powders)
- **Flavor Powders:** Salt, pudina, peri-peri, cheese, caramel, etc.
    

**Method:**

1. Mix oil + flavor + binder in a tumbler.
2. Add roasted makhana & rotate for 1–2 min to coat evenly.
3. Air-dry or tumble-dry for 10–15 min.
    

**Pro tip:** Maintain flavor particle size < 200 µm for even coating.

## 4. Cooling & Moisture Control

- Cool to **ambient (25 °C)** before packing.
- Target **final moisture < 4 %** for long shelf life.
- Use a **dehumidifier room** if possible.

## 5. Packaging

- **Material:** Multi-layer laminated pouch (metalized polyester + LDPE).
- **Options:** Vacuum seal / nitrogen flush to avoid oxidation.
- **Pack sizes:** 25 g / 60 g / 100 g / 250 g.
- **Label:** FSSAI No., nutrition table, batch code, MRP, date, allergen info.

## 7. Small-Batch (Home) Method

If you’re prototyping:

1. Heat a thick-bottom pan.
2. Add 1 tsp ghee for ~100 g makhana.
3. Roast on medium flame × 10 min — keep stirring till crisp.
4. Mix seasoning (salt, masala, etc.) while still warm.
5. Cool + store in airtight jar.

## Optimization Tips

- Use a **moisture meter** to check dryness before roasting.
- Invest in a **rotary or air-roaster** for consistency.    
- Trial with **different oils** — rice-bran gives neutral taste, ghee gives premium aroma.
- Add **flavor encapsulation** for longer taste stability if scaling industrially.

## 1. What is Makhana?

Makhana (also called **Phool Makhana** or **Fox Nuts**) are **puffed seeds** of the **Euryale ferox** plant — a water lily that grows naturally in ponds and wetlands, especially in **Bihar, Eastern UP, and Nepal**.

The seeds are **not directly edible** — they have to go through multiple thermal and mechanical processing stages to become the light, white, crunchy pops we eat.

---

## 🌾 2. Cultivation Stage

| Step            | Process                                                                         | Details                                                        |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Seed Sowing** | Makhana seeds are sown in natural ponds or artificial wetlands around May–June. | Each plant grows large circular leaves that float on water.    |
| **Maturity**    | Seeds mature after 6–8 months, around September–October.                        | Seeds stay inside the spongy fruit under water.                |
| **Harvesting**  | Divers (called “Guriyas”) collect the fruit manually from the pond bottom.      | This is one of the toughest manual farming processes in India. |

## 3. Post-Harvest Processing – Turning Seeds into Pops

This is the **core technical process** that transforms the black makhana seed into the edible white puff.

### Step-by-step breakdown:

| Stage                         | Description                                                                                                                     | Temperature / Time       |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **1. Washing & Cleaning**     | Seeds are washed to remove mud, leaves, and organic impurities.                                                                 | —                        |
| **2. Drying (Sun Drying)**    | Seeds are spread and dried for 2–3 days until moisture drops below 10–12%.                                                      | ~35–40°C                 |
| **3. Roasting (Pre-heating)** | Seeds are roasted in a **cast iron pan (Kadhai)** over high heat.                                                               | 250–300°C for 2–3 min    |
| **4. Popping (Manual)**       | After roasting, each seed is **hand-popped** by hitting it with a wooden hammer. The inner white starch expands and bursts out. | Immediate after roasting |
| **5. Separation**             | The popped white portion is separated from the unpopped shells.                                                                 | —                        |
| **6. Grading & Sorting**      | Sorted by size (A, B, C grades) and color uniformity.                                                                           | —                        |

👉 The popping success rate depends on **temperature precision and moisture content**.  
Too little moisture = seeds won’t puff.  
Too much = they’ll burn without popping.

## 4. Industrial / Modern Production (Automated)

For large-scale plants, these steps are **mechanized** to improve consistency and reduce labor cost:

1. **Mechanical Roaster (Rotary Drum / Air Roaster)** – provides uniform heating.
2. **Automatic Popping Machine** – replaces manual hammering using controlled pressure and heat.
3. **Air Separator** – separates popped vs. unpopped makhana.
4. **Grading Machine** – sorts by size using sieves.
5. **Flavor Coating Machine (Tumbler)** – for seasoning.  
6. **Nitrogen Flush Packaging Line** – ensures long shelf life.

## 5. Roasting & Flavoring (Snack Processing)

This is where **raw makhana → ready-to-eat snack** happens.

|Step|Details|
|---|---|
|**Dry Roast**|Puffed makhana is roasted again at 180–200°C for crispness.|
|**Flavoring**|Oil/ghee base (2–3%), seasoning (salt, spices, cheese, chocolate).|
|**Cooling**|Brings moisture to <4% to prevent sogginess.|
|**Packaging**|Nitrogen-sealed pouches, usually 25g–250g packs.|

## 7. By-Products & Waste Utilization

- **Unpopped seeds** can be reused for secondary roasting.
    
- **Shells and husks** are used as fish feed or compost.
    
- **Defective makhana** (broken or discolored) used in flour for sweets/snack powders.


## 8. Key Technology Factors

|Factor|Ideal Control|
|---|---|
|Moisture before roasting|10–12%|

Maintaining these precisely determines **puff size, whiteness, and crunch**.
